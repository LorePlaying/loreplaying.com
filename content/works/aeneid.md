## Story and context

Virgil's Latin epic follows Aeneas after the destruction of Troy. He escapes with his father Anchises, his son Ascanius, and the household gods, carrying both the memory of a lost home and the obligation to establish a future for his people. Storms and divine interventions redirect the Trojan survivors across the Mediterranean.

The journey brings Aeneas to Carthage, where his relationship with Dido makes the conflict between private attachment and public duty painfully clear. Later, guided by the Sibyl, he descends to the underworld and encounters his father's vision of Rome's future. In Latium, the journey gives way to war and settlement. The poem thus connects a refugee's search for a home with Rome's foundation story, while repeatedly exposing the human cost of that destiny.

## Reading the route

The itinerary links places named in the poem, from Troy and the Aegean islands to Carthage, Cumae, and the Tiber. Some stops correspond to identifiable ancient or modern sites; island-level and river-mouth points remain approximate. The route is a schematic reading aid, not an exact reconstruction of Aeneas's legendary voyage.
