## Story and context

Beowulf is an Old English heroic poem preserved in a manuscript copied around the year 1000. Its story looks back to a world of Scandinavian peoples and dynastic conflict. The Geatish warrior Beowulf first crosses the sea to help the Danish king Hrothgar, whose hall Heorot is terrorized by Grendel. After defeating Grendel, Beowulf also confronts Grendel's mother in her underwater lair.

The poem then moves forward by many decades. Beowulf has become king of the Geats when a dragon threatens his people. He wins the final fight but is mortally wounded, and his people prepare a funeral that reflects both his stature and their uncertain future. The poem balances heroic reputation with an awareness that even a great ruler cannot outlast death.

## Reading the journey

The map follows the sequence from Geatland to the Danish shore and Heorot, then back across the sea before the dragon episode. Geatland and the Danish settings are regional approximations. The Dragons Barrow is a schematic point based on the route source, not a verified archaeological site; the lines likewise represent a literary route rather than exact historical tracks.
