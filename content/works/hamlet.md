## Story and context

In Shakespeare's tragedy, Prince Hamlet returns from his studies to a Danish court altered by his father's death and his mother's marriage to Claudius. The ghost of the former king accuses Claudius of murder. Hamlet responds with suspicion and delay, staging a play to test the accusation while the court watches him for signs of treason or madness.

The tragedy's inward questions are matched by outward consequences. Hamlet's uncertainty affects Ophelia, Laertes, Gertrude, and the state itself. A voyage toward England, a pirate attack, and Hamlet's return reshape the action, but the play's principal setting remains the court at Elsinore. Its final fencing match turns private revenge into the destruction of the royal household.

## Reading the route

Wittenberg, Lübeck, Elsinore, and a regional North Sea point represent the route source's interpretation of Hamlet's movements. The play does not provide a complete travel itinerary, and the sea marker is schematic. Helsingør and Kronborg Castle are the conventional real-world association for Shakespeare's fictional Elsinore.
