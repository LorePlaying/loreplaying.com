## Story and context

Ernest Hemingway's novella follows Santiago, an aging Cuban fisherman who has gone many days without a catch. He rows alone beyond the waters near his village and hooks a great marlin. The fish tows his small skiff through a prolonged contest of endurance, during which Santiago speaks to the marlin with respect even as he tries to bring it alongside.

The return journey is another test. Sharks attack the marlin, and Santiago reaches shore with little more than its skeleton. The novella treats loss without making the effort meaningless: Santiago's skill, patience, and regard for the natural world remain visible even when the outcome is materially poor. The story's spare style places physical action beside the old man's memories, fatigue, and private resolve.

## Reading the route

Cojímar is the real fishing village associated with Santiago's home. The Gulf Stream stop represents a broad current region, not a specified fishing coordinate; the novella does not give a precise location for the marlin encounter. The short out-and-back line is therefore illustrative rather than navigational.
