## Story and context

Macbeth is Shakespeare's tragedy of ambition, political violence, and the fear that follows an unlawful seizure of power. After a successful battle, Macbeth encounters three witches whose predictions make kingship seem possible. Encouraged by Lady Macbeth, he murders Duncan and becomes king, but the act does not secure his position. It makes him dependent on further violence.

As Macbeth grows more isolated, Scotland becomes a landscape of suspicion and loss. Macduff's family is killed, Malcolm gathers support in England, and the prophecies appear to promise Macbeth safety. Their language proves dangerously ambiguous: the image of Birnam Wood moving toward Dunsinane comes true when soldiers carry branches as camouflage. The ending exposes the gap between Macbeth's confidence and the limits of his power.

## Reading the route

The map follows the source sequence through Forres, Inverness, Scone, Fife, England, Birnam Wood, and Dunsinane. The play names broad regions and dramatic settings, not a complete itinerary. In particular, the English refuge is unspecified; its marker near London is a schematic choice rather than a claim about Malcolm's exact location.
