## Story and context

Charles Dickens's historical novel opens with the return of Dr. Manette, released after years of imprisonment in the Bastille. His daughter Lucie helps him rebuild a life in London, where they become connected to Charles Darnay and the barrister Sydney Carton. The story moves between London and Paris as political tension grows into the French Revolution.

Dickens contrasts domestic care with public upheaval. In Paris, the Defarges' wine shop becomes a center of revolutionary activity, while the aristocratic violence that preceded the Revolution remains part of the novel's moral account. The plot's final exchange of identities makes sacrifice its central act: Carton's decision is both personal and bound up with the book's language of resurrection and renewal.

## Reading the route

The map moves from London through Dover and Calais to the Saint-Antoine district and the square once known as Place de la Revolution. These locations connect the novel's two cities and its revolutionary climax. Route lines show a schematic itinerary; they do not represent exact historical travel paths.
