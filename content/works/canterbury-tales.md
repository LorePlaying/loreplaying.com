## Story and context

Geoffrey Chaucer's Canterbury Tales is a Middle English collection built around a travelling company. Pilgrims meet at the Tabard Inn in Southwark and agree to tell stories on their journey to Canterbury. The frame gives Chaucer room to bring together speakers of different ranks, occupations, and temperaments, each with a distinctive voice and view of society.

The tales range from courtly romance and saintly legend to fabliau, sermon, and comic animal story. Their speakers sometimes interrupt, criticize, or answer one another, so the collection's social world extends beyond any single tale. The pilgrimage provides a shared direction, but not a single unified account: the surviving work is unfinished, and the promised storytelling contest never reaches its conclusion.

## Reading the route

The mapped sequence follows the source itinerary east through Southwark, Greenwich, and the Kent towns toward Canterbury. It is the frame journey of the pilgrims, not a route that every tale follows. The stops identify districts and towns; the line is a schematic road path rather than a surveyed medieval track.
