## Story and context

Mark Twain's novel follows Tom Sawyer through mischief, games, friendship, and danger in a fictional Mississippi River town. Tom turns ordinary chores into opportunities for performance, and his adventures with Huck Finn and Becky Thatcher move between school, the river, and the surrounding countryside. The story's comic energy sits beside moments of fear, especially when Tom and Huck witness a crime and must decide whether to speak.

The town of St. Petersburg draws on Twain's childhood home of Hannibal, Missouri, but it remains a fictional setting. The novel's childhood games also lead into more serious experiences: the boys run away to an island, attend their own supposed funeral, and later become lost underground. Tom's confidence and imagination help him navigate these situations, though the story does not erase the consequences of adult violence.

## Reading the route

Hannibal is shown as a real-world model for St. Petersburg, and Mark Twain Cave is the traditional identification for McDougal's Cave. Jackson's Island is left without a map pin because the novel does not establish a verified real-world island for the episode. The route therefore distinguishes a literary setting from the places that can be responsibly mapped.
