## Story and context

Herman Melville's novel begins with Ishmael's decision to go to sea and follows him aboard the whaling ship Pequod. Captain Ahab has made the hunt for Moby Dick the purpose of the voyage, drawing the crew into an increasingly dangerous pursuit. Ishmael narrates the voyage, but the book also ranges widely through whaling practice, marine life, theatrical speeches, sermons, and reflections on knowledge.

The Pequod's crew brings together people from many places and traditions. Their labor at sea becomes a setting for questions about authority, fate, belief, and the limits of human understanding. Ahab's determination narrows the world to one target, while Ishmael's narration remains expansive and attentive to contradictions. The final chase destroys the ship and its crew, leaving Ishmael as the sole survivor and storyteller.

## Reading the voyage

The route follows the whaling grounds and ocean passages named in the source map, from Nantucket across the Atlantic and around the Cape of Good Hope to the Pacific. Many points are broad whaling regions rather than precise coordinates. The map's lines are sampled, schematic paths; they are not a claim about the Pequod's exact historical track.
