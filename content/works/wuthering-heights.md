## Story and context

Emily Brontë's novel is set among two households on the Yorkshire moors. The story reaches the reader through nested narrators: Lockwood rents Thrushcross Grange, while Nelly Dean recounts the intertwined histories of the Earnshaw and Linton families. Heathcliff's arrival as a child unsettles the Earnshaw home, and his attachment to Catherine becomes entangled with resentment, class, and revenge.

The book moves across generations rather than following a simple chronological line. Its changing narrators and shifts in time complicate what can be known about the characters. The moorland setting is more than scenery: exposure, distance, weather, and the contrast between the two houses shape the novel's emotional atmosphere. The later generation offers a partial interruption of the destructive pattern inherited from their parents.

## Reading the route

Wuthering Heights, Thrushcross Grange, Penistone Crags, and Gimmerton belong to the novel's fictional landscape. The source map places them in a schematic Yorkshire setting, but the atlas deliberately leaves them without precise pins. The ordered itinerary is useful for following the story's geography without presenting invented coordinates as real locations.
